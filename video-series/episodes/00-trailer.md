## Script

### 0.1 Hook · ≈ 0:20

**Visual.** [V0.1a] Black canvas. A single white dot appears in the centre and pulses
once. [V0.1b] It stretches into a line, the line curls into a circle, the circle
creases into a square, and the square spins and folds into a five-pointed star.
Every motion is smooth and unhurried.

> Every animation you have ever watched in a math video is, underneath, a list of instructions.
>
> Put a dot here. Turn it into a circle. Now fold that circle into a square. <break time="0.6s" />
>
> What if you could write those instructions yourself, and watch them come alive the moment you finish typing?

### 0.2 The reveal · ≈ 0:30

**Visual.** [V0.2a] The star slides right; on the left a code panel types out the
`Showcase` scene from q00-01, line by line, in step with the narration. [V0.2b] Cut
to a screen recording of ManimOnline: the same code in the editor, the preview playing
on the right. [V0.2c] The cursor changes `RED` to `GREEN` on the `Star` line; one
second later the preview re-renders with a green star.

> This is Manim, the Python library behind a whole genre of animated math explanations. And this is ManimOnline, which runs a Manim-compatible engine entirely inside your web browser. No install. No server doing the work. You stop typing, and a moment later, it plays.

**QR q00-01** appears at [V0.2b] and stays to the end of segment 0.3.

### 0.3 What the series covers · ≈ 0:30

**Visual.** [V0.3a] A fast montage, about two seconds per shot, drawn from later
episodes: the de Casteljau construction tracing a curve (ep 4); `2x + 3 = 11` solving
itself (ep 5); a circle unrolling into a sine wave (ep 6); Riemann rectangles
thinning into an area (ep 7); a surface turning in 3D (ep 8); a grid of 512 squares
flickering between two hash fingerprints (ep 12). [V0.3b] The montage collapses into
the series title card: "Drawing with Code".

> In this series we will start from a single square, and build up to curves, equations, graphs, cameras that fly through three dimensions, and animations that respond to their own changing numbers.
>
> Then, for the curious, we will open the hood: how Python can run in a browser tab at all, how you rebuild an animation engine and prove that it behaves the same as the original, and how a link, or a QR code like this one, can carry an entire program.

### 0.4 Call to action · ≈ 0:10

**Visual.** [V0.4a] Title card holds. The QR code grows slightly and a caption
reads "Scan to edit this trailer's final scene".

> Every few minutes, a code like this will appear in the corner. Scan it, and the exact scene on screen opens in your browser, ready for you to change. <break time="0.5s" /> Let's begin.
