# Production graphics

Manim Community scenes for the series' visuals, one file per episode
(`epNN.py`) and one Scene class per script segment (`Segment_1_5` is segment 1.5).
Shot ids in comments (`[V1.5b]`) match the **Visual.** notes in `../episodes/`.

Episodes 1–5 are complete; episode 1 is the template for the others. Episode 1 is
narrated (see Narration).

## Requirements

- Manim Community 0.21 or later (`manim --version`). Production does not use manim-lite.
- A TeX installation with `latex` and `dvisvgm` for `MathTex`. `render.py` adds
  `~/Library/TinyTeX/bin/universal-darwin` to the PATH when it exists and `latex` isn't found.
- The **Avenir Next** font (ships with macOS). Set `SERIES_FONT` to another installed
  family on other systems; Pango otherwise falls back to a serif face.
- ffmpeg (installed with Manim) for stitching.
- For the QR check only: a Python with `opencv-python-headless`.

## Rendering

```bash
python3 video-series/production/render.py 1
```

renders every segment of episode 1 at 1080p60 and stitches a silent animatic to
`build/episodes/ep01-1080p60.mp4`. Add `-ql` for a quick 480p15 review render, or list
segments (`render.py 1 -ql 1.5 1.6`) to render only those. Segment videos are in
`build/media/videos/ep01/<quality>/`, which is the folder to import into the editor.
Set `SEGMENT_LABELS=1` to burn the segment id and clock into the top-left corner for review.

## Timing

`common.Segment` sets each scene's length from its segment's narration: words at 145 per
minute plus any `<break/>` pauses, read straight from the episode script. Visual beats
are placed with `self.hold_until(fraction)` at the point in the narration where they
belong, and `self.finish()` holds until the end.

`tools/narrate.py` measures each generated segment and writes
`build/narration/durations.json`, for example `{"01.1": 33.0, "01.10": 37.7}` (keys are
`EE.S`, the episode padded to two digits). Re-rendering then fits every scene to the
real audio without code changes. Fractions were chosen from paragraph word counts, so
small drifts within a segment are expected; adjust a `hold_until` if a beat lands early.

## Narration

`python3 video-series/tools/narrate.py N` generates episode N's narration (ElevenLabs,
voice "Curt Cox 2", key from the keychain). After that, `render.py N` re-times the scenes
and the stitched file carries the soundtrack: each segment's audio starts with its scene
and is padded with silence to the scene's length (scenes run 0.8 s past their audio, more
when a fixed beat such as the 10-second QR wall needs it). Because the voice is faster than
the 145 wpm estimate, re-check QR card times after narrating an episode: each card should
stay up at least 8 s.

## Conventions

- **Colour roles** (`common.py`): `OBJECT` blue, `FOCUS` yellow, `AFTER` green,
  `WARNING` red, `SCAFFOLD` grey.
- **On-screen code** always comes from the QR snippet itself (`snippet_source`,
  `snippet_lines` in common.py), so it can't drift from what the QR code opens. Use
  `code_panel` and `line_highlight`.
- **QR codes** are drawn by `qr_card`/`qr_wall` as vector squares from
  `qr-matrices.json` (generated by `tools/make_qr_svgs.py`). Don't load the SVG files:
  Manim's SVG import drops segno's stroked module paths and shows a blank square.
  Cards sit bottom-right at 25 % of frame height; walls use rows of three.
  `python video-series/production/check_qr.py` renders every card at 1080p and decodes it;
  `check_qr.py --wall N` decodes each code on episode N's rendered closing wall.
  Long QR titles wrap onto two lines (`wrapped_text`) rather than shrinking.
- `anchors_and_handles` (common.py) marks every cubic of a VMobject, letters and formulas included.
- **Screen recordings** are `footage_card` placeholders listing the shots to capture.
  Never draw an imitation of the ManimOnline interface; replace the card with real
  footage in the edit. Error and code panels (as in 1.9) are diagrams, not UI mock-ups.
- Regenerate `qr-matrices.json` whenever a snippet changes (its CID, and so its QR code,
  changes too): `node video-series/tools/qr-links.mjs` then
  `python video-series/tools/make_qr_svgs.py`.

## Episode 1 notes

- 1.1 replays the real 15 fps preview frames of q01-01 (46 of them) one at a time, then
  fans eleven of them into a flipbook.
- 1.2 shows the four sentences before the progress slider, following the narration's
  order (the script's visual note lists the slider first).
- 1.4 and 1.7 open with footage cards for the editor recordings; 1.7 continues with the
  annotated q01-02 code.
- 1.8's timeline blocks grow for exactly the duration of the play or wait they stand for.
- 1.9 reproduces the real Pyodide traceback lines verified in the browser.
- 1.10 ends with the 10-second QR wall and the episode 2 teaser.

## Episode 2 notes

- 2.2 fakes the depth hint with a 2D tilt of the plane (`ApplyMatrix`) and an `OUT`
  arrow; real 3D waits for episode 8. Its QR card shows twice: after the vector
  walk and again for the closing sentence that mentions it.
- 2.4 drives stroke width, fill colour and opacity from `ValueTracker` sliders.
- 2.5 annotates each positioning verb (offset arrow, target crosshair, dashed bounding
  boxes and buffer, frame margin, alignment guide) and ends with jump-versus-glide and
  a `next_to` label that follows its shape while a `move_to` label stays behind.
- 2.8 ties sticky-note names to squares with updaters; 2.9 shows drawing order as a
  side-view stack of sheets that re-stacks as `z_index` changes.

## Episode 3 notes

- 3.1 and 3.5 freeze `.animate.rotate` halfway with a `Transform` whose rate function stops at
  0.5 (`halfway()`), then finish it with a second `Transform` to the same target. That second
  half is exactly the rest of the straight-line path.
- 3.2 and 3.5 draw the interpolated shapes from a `ValueTracker` alpha (corner lerps), so
  each corner's dashed track and the 71 % midway square are exact, not illustrated.
- 3.3 rides each rate function's dot on its own lane while a matching dot traces its graph;
  the closing race reproduces the q03-01 layout, moved up for the QR card.
- 3.4, 3.6 and 3.7 replay their snippets' own animations, captioned by their source lines.
- 3.6's timing bars are drawn to scale (2.1 units per second) with a playhead that sweeps
  in step with the real `AnimationGroup`, `LaggedStart` and `Succession` calls.

## Episode 4 notes

- `lerp_levels`/`scaffold` draw de Casteljau's construction for any number of control
  points (4.2, 4.6, 4.8, 4.9); `curve_upto` is the partial curve Create shows at t.
- 4.1 zooms by scaling about the circle's edge (×10, ×100, ×1000): Manim's eight cubics stay
  smooth while the 24-gon shows its corner at ×10.
- 4.5 prints `Square(3).get_points()` itself, so the listed coordinates are the real ones.
- 4.6 splits a cubic at t = ½ from the frozen scaffold, then calls `align_points` on the
  q04-03 square so the new anchors are Manim's own.
- 4.7's dashed tracks sample `path_along_arc` directly, the same function `path_arc` uses.
  Its pairing demo reverses one circle: halfway through, the outline flattens.

## Episode 5 notes

- Formulas come from Manim's own TeX pipeline, so production needs TeX (see Requirements).
- `chars`/`find_chars` pick a Text's glyphs by source position (Text has no glyphs for
  spaces), for colouring parts of code lines; `isolated` returns the glyphs that
  `tex_to_color_map`/`substrings_to_isolate` isolated. In Manim 0.21 an isolated substring
  doesn't split the formula into parts: it is a glyph group in `id_to_vgroup_dict`.
- `TERM_COLORS` gives each term of the q05-03 working one colour everywhere: the code chips,
  their match lines (5.6), the full-screen steps and the recap.
- 5.1 traces 2x and = with trails beneath the formula while TransformMatchingTex glides them.
- 5.6 replays the second step in slow motion, labelling the unmatched + and the new −.
- 5.7 moves the lines under the first equals sign; the red guides slide with them and merge.
- 5.9 uses the q05-05 figure (sides 2.4 and 1.6, so the hypotenuse square stays in frame).
